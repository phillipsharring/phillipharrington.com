## Post: "Changes Go Forward" (idea, 2026-11-16)

The same principle keeps turning up in three places:

- **Reuse Lists:** Reuse makes a fresh version; the checklist you finished stays
  exactly as it was. The past is an artifact, not a draft. Changes go forward.
- **Migrations:** dropping `down()` from handlr. You don't un-run history; you
  write the next migration. Roll forward only.
- **Seed scenarios** ("Everybody Knows Alice"): never change what scenario 12
  means. Need something different? That's 12b, or a new number. Old tickets,
  tests and bug reports keep meaning what they said.

Probably also: git itself (commits are append-only, revert is a new commit),
changelogs as dated records rather than rewritten history, the "say it once"
post's grandfathering. Check before claiming more.

**Where:** here, as the philosophy piece. Reuse Lists' blog can carry a
product-angled version that links back. Write it after "Everybody Knows Alice"
is out, and link the two.
