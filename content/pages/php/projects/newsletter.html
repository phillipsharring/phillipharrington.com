Newsletter Phillip Harrington

Queues sent one email to one person who had just signed up. A newsletter sends the same email to everyone on a list, again and again. The whole difficulty is the list: who's on it, and whether they asked to be.

A signup form takes an email address, but it can't know whose address it is. Anyone can type yours. You'll build the naive form, use it to fill a stranger's inbox and put them on your list without asking, and then build the one that only ever mails people who proved the address is theirs, and lets them leave with one click.

What You'll Use
  • Sending Email and Queues: Symfony Mailer and Mailpit, as before
  • Never Trust Input: an address typed by someone who may not own it
  • Rate Limiting: per address, and per visitor
  • Comments: the honeypot, and signing with hash_hmac()
  • Passwords: store a hash of a secret token, never the token
    • Build It Step 1: The Setup

    The containers from Queues, without the worker, and with two more settings: the site's address for links, and a secret for signing unsubscribe links. composer.json, composer.lock, the Dockerfile and src/Database.php are the same too.

    docker run --rm -v "$PWD":/app -w /app composer:2 composer install

    Two tables: the subscribers, and a log of every email the signup form sends, which is what the limits count:

    A subscriber starts pending. Only a person clicking the link in their own inbox makes them confirmed, and only confirmed addresses ever get an issue. This is called double opt-in: once on the form, once from the inbox.

    Step 2: The Rules

    All the decisions live in one class, src/Subscriptions.php:

  • One confirmation per address per day. However many times the address is typed in, its owner gets one email, and it says "if this wasn't you, ignore it."
  • Five per visitor per hour. Stops one visitor from working through a list of strangers' addresses.
  • Nothing to already-confirmed addresses. And the page says the same thing either way, so the form can't be used to find out who's subscribed.
  • A random token, stored as a hash. The token is only ever in the email. Someone who reads your database can't confirm addresses with what they find there.
  • A signed unsubscribe link. The subscriber's id, signed with hash_hmac() and the secret, so it works forever but nobody can make one for someone else's id.

    Step 4: Sending an Issue

    An issue is a text file, subject on the first line:

    bin/send.php sends it to confirmed subscribers and nobody else. Every copy carries an unsubscribe link in the text, and two headers that mail programs turn into an Unsubscribe button: List-Unsubscribe, with the link, and List-Unsubscribe-Post, which says one POST to it is enough.

    Step 5: Run It docker compose up

    Subscribe at http://localhost:8000 three times with the same address. Open Mailpit at http://localhost:8025: one email. Open its link, press the button, and you're confirmed. Then, from a second terminal, send the issue:

    Same as before: exec app runs the command inside the app container that's already running.

    Break It

    The signup form most people write first:

    It adds whatever address it's given, as subscribed, and emails it at once. Be a stranger:

    for i in $(seq 1 10); do curl -s -d email=victim@example.com http://localhost:8000/naive.php done

    Open Mailpit. victim@example.com has ten emails they never asked for, and is on your list, marked subscribed, to get every issue from now on. Point the loop at a list of real addresses, and your server is now sending mail to thousands of people who never heard of you. Their mail providers mark you as spam, your own mail stops arriving anywhere, and the people who got it can't make it stop.

    Fix It

    Now the same loop against the real form:

    for i in $(seq 1 10); do curl -s -o /dev/null -d email=victim@example.com http://localhost:8000/ done

    One email, asking the owner to confirm, and telling them to ignore it if it wasn't them. The address is pending, so no issue will ever go to it. Try ten different addresses instead, and once five emails have gone out for you in an hour, the form answers 429 Too Many Requests. Fill in the hidden company field, the way a bot would, and the page says thanks and sends nothing.

    By now you could teach this part. Never trust input, and an email address is an odd kind of input: it's a claim about someone else. Until the owner answers from their own inbox, it's just a string someone typed.

    One of my apps had a signup form that sent a verification email to whatever address was typed in. Someone found it and used it to flood strangers' inboxes: sign up a victim's address, over and over, and the app sent each one a verification email. The app's own mail was the attack. The fix was to send nothing at signup, put rate limits on mail, and add a bot check. Repeated side story: the signup inbox bomb story is also told in Rate Limiting. Keep one, make this a callback, or cut it. Gotchas
  • The confirmation email is mail too. It's the one email you have to send to an address you can't trust, so it's the one the limits are for. Without them, double opt-in is just a slower way to bomb an inbox.
  • Big senders must offer one-click unsubscribe. Gmail and Yahoo have required List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post from bulk senders since 2024. Without them, your mail goes to spam or doesn't arrive.
  • Unsubscribe means at once. No login, no "are you sure" questions beyond one button, no "it may take ten days." In many places the law requires a working unsubscribe; in some, proof of consent too. Keep the dates in the table.
  • Sending to thousands is a job for the queue. A loop that sends every email in one script works for a small list. For a big one, queue a job per subscriber, and use a mail service built for bulk sending.
  • Bounces and complaints mean stop. An address that bounces, or a subscriber who marks you as spam, should come off the list. Mail services report both; listen to them.

    • Many mail providers deliver you+news@example.com to you@example.com. Subscribe your own list with a new plus address for each test, and you can test the whole flow, confirm, send and unsubscribe, against your real inbox as often as you like.
