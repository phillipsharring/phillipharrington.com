Taking a Payment with Stripe Checkout Phillip Harrington

Checkout left an order awaiting payment, and a promise: the card number goes to the payment provider, never to you. Here's how that works. Your server tells Stripe what the order costs. Stripe gives back the address of a payment page on its own servers. The customer types their card there. Afterward, Stripe tells your server, in a signed message, that the money arrived.

Everything here runs in Stripe's test mode, with test keys and Stripe's published test card numbers. No real money moves, and the example refuses to start with a live key.

    Card numbers end in a check digit, worked out with a formula patented by Hans Peter Luhn, an IBM engineer, in 1960. Double every second digit from the right, add up the digits, and the total of a valid number ends in 0. It catches most typos, which is why a payment form can say "that number's wrong" before asking anyone. Stripe's test numbers, 4242 4242 4242 4242 included, pass the check, so they look valid to any form, and in test mode Stripe treats each one as a card with a known outcome. Gotchas
  • Read the raw body. The signature covers the exact bytes Stripe sent. Decode and re-encode the JSON before checking, and the bytes change and the check fails. php://input, untouched, first.
  • Test and live are separate worlds. Different keys, different webhook secrets, different customers and orders. A test key can't move real money, which is why it's the only kind this example accepts.
  • Fulfill from the webhook, not the redirect. Ship the order, send the receipt, grant the download: all of it when the webhook says paid. A customer who closes the tab on Stripe's page has still paid.
  • Answer the webhook fast. Stripe waits only a short while. Slow work, like emailing a receipt, goes in a queue, and the webhook answers 200 at once.
  • Abandoned sessions are normal. Many people get to the payment page and leave. The order stays awaiting payment, and the cron job that cancels old unpaid orders handles it.
    • One of my own apps takes its payments exactly this way, through Stripe Checkout. No card number ever touches its server, so there's no card number on it to lose.

    Have your code check the key's prefix, the way stripeTestKey() does, in every environment that isn't production. A development machine that refuses sk_live_ can't charge a real card because someone pasted the wrong key into the wrong .env.
